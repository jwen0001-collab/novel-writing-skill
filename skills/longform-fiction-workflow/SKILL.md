# Longform Fiction Workflow

Use this workflow when the work must remain coherent over multiple scenes, chapters, or installments. It is genre-neutral: the project’s own canon and style guide always outrank these defaults.

## Establish the writing contract first

Before drafting, locate or create only the documents the project needs:

- **Story facts:** chronology, character knowledge, relationships, locations, objects, unresolved promises, and irreversible events.
- **Voice contract:** point of view, diction, sentence rhythm, dialogue distance, imagery, taboo phrases or devices, and how emotion is externalized.
- **Narrative contract:** what each installment must change, what the ending may not resolve, and what counts as a credible cost.

Keep facts separate from preferences. A canon record answers *what is true*; a style guide answers *how it is told*; an outline answers *what must happen next*. Do not bury permanent facts in a one-off drafting prompt.

If an established work lacks these materials, first make a compact continuity sheet and a voice sheet from the existing text. Do not invent missing facts merely to make a plan neat.

## Plan at two levels

For a volume or arc, make a short arc outline that states the central pressure, the irreversible turn, the ending state, and explicit non-outcomes. Then give every chapter or story a card before prose is commissioned.

Each card should specify:

- viewpoint, time, and place;
- scene mode and emotional temperature, so neighboring installments do not become mechanically alike;
- the initial debt or pressure;
- a visible local payoff, expressed as a changed action, possession, access, relationship, or public record;
- the reversal or cost that prevents easy closure;
- one physical or behavioral emotional beat;
- mandatory concrete elements and prohibited outcomes.

Use a five-beat shape when useful: establish a debt, apply pressure, produce a small payoff, impose a reversal or cost, and leave a new debt. It is a diagnostic tool, not a required formula. For literary, experimental, or quiet fiction, preserve the same causal clarity without forcing conventional escalation.

Read [the chapter-card reference](references/chapter-cards.md) only when creating a new project template or when a team needs a stricter handoff format.

## Delegate prose without losing authorship

When multiple writers or agents are available, give each a non-overlapping installment and the smallest sufficient brief: relevant canon slice, style constraints, chapter card, allowed length, target file, and explicit prohibitions. Do not send the full project archive by default.

The lead writer retains responsibility for continuity and quality. Writers may draft, but they must not silently revise shared canon or other installments. Parallel drafting is safe only after the arc logic, time ordering, and object/knowledge handoffs are locked.

For each delivered draft, check title and format, length, mechanical bans, continuity, and then read it as a scene. A clean lint result is never a substitute for literary review.

## Review in the right order

Review for structural errors before polishing sentences:

1. **Continuity:** time, possession, location, injuries, information, names, and authority match the record.
2. **Causality and agency:** every material change has an actor, a mechanism, a beneficiary, and a cost; supporting characters may refuse, bargain, or pursue their own work.
3. **Dramatic pressure:** exposition is embedded in conflict, procedure, desire, or a task; the scene does not merely restate prior knowledge.
4. **Voice and emotion:** abstract emotional conclusions are replaced where possible by action, sensory detail, silence, interruption, or an object under pressure.
5. **Line-level compliance:** punctuation, forbidden stock phrasing, formatting, repeated images, and target length.

Fix the smallest passage that resolves the actual fault. Update the appropriate shared record whenever a draft creates a new permanent fact, closes a promise, or changes the future options of a character.

## Ending and sequel discipline

An ending may leave obligations, disputed interpretations, and ongoing material conditions. Do not solve every relationship, institutional problem, or social harm merely because the arc ends. Conversely, do not manufacture a sequel hook that contradicts the ending state.

For an epilogue or afterword, choose a concrete after-effect of the ending rather than reopening the main antagonist or central mystery. Preserve the original ending’s non-outcomes unless the user explicitly asks to change them.

## Completion record

For a substantial arc, save a concise audit note that records chapters delivered, major corrections, unresolved boundaries, and the facts later writers must not overwrite. This is a handoff document, not a celebratory summary.
